# Pass 7 — selective visual analysis

**Status: first visual review complete for all 23 Pass 6 windows; seven received a closer five-second sequence review.** This pass tests six provisional relationships against the edited footage and timed captions. It does not constitute a full replay reconstruction or a corpus-wide validation.

## Method and audit boundary

The 23 silent clips cover 52 minutes 26 seconds from 12 videos. I inspected a timestamped ten-second visual sweep of every window, read the caption segments within each window, and inspected five-second, larger-frame sequences in V6-02, V6-07, V6-08, V6-14, V6-18, V6-21, and V6-23. The source time labels refer to the YouTube upload time, not the match clock. `pass07_visual_ledger.jsonl` records one visual observation, the separate caption interpretation, an assessment, and an explicit limit for each window. `pass07_contact_sheets.py` reproduces the initial frame sweep from the archived clips. The Pass 6 CSV now distinguishes `reviewed_sampled` (16) from `reviewed_targeted` (7); neither means a complete frame-by-frame or independent match-VOD audit.

This distinction matters because much of the source is a coached video: long frozen replay frames, drawn arrows, a nested YouTube player, POV switches, and cuts between fights. An annotation is evidence of what the speaker wants to show, not independent evidence that an opponent had a particular belief. The 480p clips sometimes make roster cooldown icons too small to count reliably. Captions are automatically generated and occasionally garble names and abilities. I used the visible HUD, objective, movement, and kill feed where legible; I did not turn narrated causal claims into observed facts.

## Cross-question model candidates for Pass 8

The same decision structure recurs across these six topics: **position and objective pressure create a menu of opponent responses; resource timing and information determine whether the next response is punishable; a team must preserve a useful second move.** This is a candidate unification of the transcript clusters, not a demonstrated law of play. The visual examples support the existence of these mechanisms in selected fights; the edited footage does not estimate their frequency or superiority across the channel.

Three distinctions should stay separate in the contradiction pass:

1. **Voluntary reset versus forced displacement.** Similar movement backward can preserve initiative or surrender the current corner, depending on distance, cooldowns, and opponent staging.
2. **Resource inventory versus conversion.** Ultimate advantage matters through the fight it can actually set up. Panic spending, an intentional multi-ultimate retake, and poor map position after an ultimate are different failure modes.
3. **Bait offered versus response controlled.** Offering a cooldown or exposed player changes the opponent's options, but does not remove alternative routes or ensure the baiting side can counterattack.
