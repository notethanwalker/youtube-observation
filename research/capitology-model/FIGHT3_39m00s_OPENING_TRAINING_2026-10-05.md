# Capitology Fight 3 Training — New Junk City, ~39:00–39:10

Status: human-adjudicated training notes in progress.

## Fight context
TM reaches the initial staging first from spawn. That initial staging advantage is probably unavoidable and is not itself the key mistake.

## CR intended plan
CR's plan is a split setup combined with Vigilante's Juno ultimate through main:
- HeeSang and the other DPS-side pressure create the pinch.
- CR's main group uses Juno ult to surge through main.
- The side players should collapse inward as the main group pushes.
- The intended conversion is to catch exposed TM targets around point during the collapse.

## Critical requirement for this plan
For a Juno-ult pinch to work, CR's DPS must EXPECT the enemy targets to try to escape.
They therefore need to:
- scout the likely targets and their exact positions,
- identify likely escape routes,
- pre-position to catch or block those exits,
- then collapse with the Juno ult.

The pinch is not complete just because CR has players on multiple sides. The escape routes must actually be covered.

## What went wrong
Quartz = Sojourn (verified via direct HUD-icon cross-reference).
Simple = Moira.

Quartz and Simple are the obvious targets CR would normally expect to punish in this setup, but neither is caught:
- Simple escapes with Fade.
- Quartz escapes with Sojourn mobility.
- No CR player is positioned to reliably catch either escape.

Primary CR failure:
- inadequate scouting of the targets,
- inadequate control of their escape routes,
- DPS setup creates inward pressure but does not seal the exits.

## TM counter
TVNT recognizes that CR is split and reads the punch play early.
Instead of waiting passively for the pinch to close, he pushes main early and pressures Vigilante/Juno.

Effect:
- Vigilante is put under pressure before CR's full setup is complete,
- the Juno ult becomes more difficult / somewhat early,
- CR is pushed into starting the engage before the DPS have fully solved the escape routes.

## Correct opening sequence
CR ideal:
split -> locate targets -> seal exits -> Juno ult -> collapse

Actual:
split -> incomplete scouting/exit control -> TVNT pressures main early -> Juno ult comes under pressure -> Quartz/Simple escape -> collapse fails

## Coaching interpretation
The problem is not simply that TM 'escaped the pinch.'
CR's chosen plan required the DPS to anticipate escape and set up to deny it.
TM also deserves credit: TVNT identifies the split and proactively disrupts the main component of the punch before the collapse is ready.

## Analysis-model lessons
1. Fight plans must be read as SETUP + ULTIMATE, not staging alone.
2. When analyzing an ultimate-based collapse, identify the intended kill targets.
3. Ask what defensive/mobility options those targets have.
4. Ask whether the attacking DPS have actually covered the escape routes.
5. Check whether the defending tank/core can disrupt the ultimate user before the setup is complete.
6. Do not over-credit geometry alone; a pinch that leaves exits open may look correct but fail structurally.
7. Hero IDs must be verified using the canonical icon reference before hero-specific reasoning.

## Fight outcome / why later phase is low-leverage
Human adjudication:
- This fight is mostly decided by the failed initial interaction.
- After the failed pinch, CR is left in awkward positions and does not have strong ultimates for a clean second plan.
- TM can then use Ramattra ultimate plus Sojourn ultimate to force CR backward.
- TM finds a kill on CR's Bastion when he drops.
- CR could theoretically fully kite the ults and attempt a re-engage, or make a chance play, but both are probably low-percentage options.
- Therefore, the useful coaching/research value is concentrated in the opening setup + Juno-ult interaction rather than overanalyzing the later cleanup.

Model lesson:
When an early interaction materially destroys position and resource quality, distinguish the decisive interaction from later low-agency cleanup. Do not assign equal analytical weight to every phase of the fight.
